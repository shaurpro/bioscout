# Green fluorescent protein (GFP)

GFP comes from the jellyfish Aequorea victoria. Its 238 residues fold into an eleven-stranded beta
barrel with a central helix. The chromophore forms on its own from three residues (Ser65-Tyr66-Gly67)
through cyclisation, dehydration and oxidation, so no external cofactor is needed apart from oxygen.

Because it is genetically encoded, GFP can be fused to almost any protein to track where that protein
goes inside living cells. Engineered variants such as EGFP (brighter, folds better at 37 C) and colour
variants (CFP, YFP) made it a standard tool, and the discovery and development of GFP was recognised
with the 2008 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
