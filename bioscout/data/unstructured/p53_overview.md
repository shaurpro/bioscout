# p53: the "guardian of the genome"

p53 (gene TP53) is a transcription factor that sits at the centre of the cell's response to stress.
Under normal conditions p53 levels are kept low because the E3 ubiquitin ligase MDM2 tags it for
degradation by the proteasome. When DNA is damaged, kinases such as ATM and ATR phosphorylate p53,
which blocks MDM2 binding. p53 then accumulates, forms a tetramer and binds DNA.

Active p53 switches on genes that pause the cell cycle (for example p21, which inhibits CDK
complexes) so the damage can be repaired. If the damage is too severe, p53 instead activates
pro-apoptotic genes such as BAX and PUMA, and the cell dies.

TP53 is the most frequently mutated gene in human cancer. Most mutations are missense changes in the
DNA-binding domain, which stop p53 from recognising its target sequences. Hotspot residues include
R175, R248 and R273.
