# EGFR signalling and targeted therapy

The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a receptor tyrosine kinase in the cell membrane. When a
ligand such as EGF binds, two receptors pair up (dimerise) and phosphorylate each other's tyrosine
residues. These phosphotyrosines recruit adaptor proteins that switch on the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK and
PI3K-AKT pathways, which drive cell growth and survival.

Activating mutations in the kinase domain, such as exon 19 deletions and L858R, keep the receptor
switched on in some non-small-cell lung cancers. Small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (gefitinib,
erlotinib, and later osimertinib, which also covers the T790M resistance mutation) block the
ATP-binding site and are used to treat these tumours.
