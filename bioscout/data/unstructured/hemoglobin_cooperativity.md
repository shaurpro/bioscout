# Hemoglobin and cooperative oxygen binding

Adult hemoglobin is a tetramer of two alpha and two beta subunits. Each subunit holds a heme group with
an iron atom that binds one oxygen molecule. Binding is cooperative: once one subunit binds oxygen,
the tetramer shifts from the low-affinity T (tense) state toward the high-affinity R (relaxed) state,
making it easier for the remaining subunits to bind. This produces the sigmoidal oxygen dissociation curve.

Allosteric effectors push the curve to the right and promote oxygen release in tissues: lower pH and
higher CO2 (the Bohr effect) and 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG).

Sickle-cell disease is caused by a single substitution in the beta chain, Glu6Val. The hydrophobic
valine lets deoxygenated hemoglobin molecules stick together into long fibres that deform red blood cells.
